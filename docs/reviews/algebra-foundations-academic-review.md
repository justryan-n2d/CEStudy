# Algebra Foundations: Pre-Lesson Academic Review

**Review date:** 2026-10-18  
**Reviewed artifact:** [Algebra Foundations syllabus v0.2](../curriculum/algebra-foundations-syllabus.md)  
**Review type:** AI-assisted academic desk review of course scope, sequence, outcome alignment, and source claims  
**Status:** Revised after desk review; independent instructor review remains outstanding  
**Decision rule:** One subject = one syllabus. Algebra Readiness remains the opening phase inside the one Algebra Foundations syllabus. No lesson or practice-question bank is part of this review.

## 1. Review question and boundaries

The review asked whether the syllabus gives a coherent, teachable College Algebra pathway; places prerequisite concepts before dependent concepts; states mathematical restrictions where they matter; keeps the approved topic boundaries; and describes external curricula and sources accurately.

This review did **not** verify future individual lessons, worked solutions, answer keys, or practice questions because those artifacts have not been written. It is not a faculty sign-off or a claim that the syllabus is an official university/CHED syllabus.

## 2. Summary verdict

**Contract verdict: Confirmed for the approved syllabus scope, with documented differences from specific university course packaging.** The document has one named subject and one syllabus, keeps Algebra Readiness as a non-blocking opening phase, preserves separate boundaries for Advanced Algebra, Trigonometry, Analytic Geometry, Solid Mensuration, and Calculus, and does not invent university credits, teaching weeks, or grading percentages.

**Academic quality verdict: Partially confirmed.** The pre-lesson desk review identified and corrected sequence and scope clarity issues. The resulting outline is more coherent for lesson development, but the quality of actual mathematical teaching, examples, solutions, questions, accessibility, and assessment thresholds cannot be confirmed until materials exist and a qualified instructor reviews them.

## 3. Findings and changes made

### A1. Complex-number prerequisites appeared too late

- **Severity:** High for instructional sequence
- **Evidence:** Previous Unit 7 discussed complex quadratic roots before Unit 8 introduced complex-number notation and operations.
- **Change:** Unit 7 now introduces the imaginary unit, a + bi notation, powers of i, and basic operations before classifying a negative-discriminant quadratic. Unit 8 uses and extends those basics for selected higher-degree polynomial equations.
- **Verification needed during lesson writing:** Explain why a negative discriminant leads to nonreal complex roots, show the relationship between square roots of negative numbers and i, and check terminology consistently.

### A2. Higher-degree polynomial equation methods were underspecified

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Evidence:** The former outline mentioned factoring, division, and known factors but did not state how students would identify possible rational roots.
- **Change:** Unit 8 now includes the Rational Root Theorem specifically for candidate rational zeros of polynomials with integer coefficients, plus polynomial/synthetic division and checking candidates in the original equation.
- **Verification needed during lesson writing:** State the integer-coefficient condition when applying the Rational Root Theorem, distinguish candidate roots from proven roots, and verify every candidate by substitution or the Factor Theorem.

### A3. Rational-function coverage was vague and could drift into Analytic Geometry

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Evidence:** “How values change” did not define the expected depth.
- **Change:** Unit 9 now limits this area to allowed inputs, excluded inputs, zeros where defined, and simple tables or graphs. Asymptotes and advanced curve behavior are explicitly outside this unit's scope.
- **Verification needed during lesson writing:** Do not imply that every rational-function graph feature or limit behavior belongs in Algebra Foundations.

### A4. Logarithm restrictions needed an explicit operational check

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Evidence:** The outline referred to logarithm restrictions but could be interpreted as a formula-only treatment.
- **Change:** Unit 11 now requires positive logarithm arguments and checking candidate solutions in the original logarithmic equation.
- **Verification needed during lesson writing:** Check the domain before and after applying logarithm rules; reject extraneous solutions produced by algebraic transformations.

### A5. A cited university's College Algebra packaging differs from CEStudy's boundary

- **Severity:** Medium, curriculum alignment
- **Evidence:** De La Salle University's published course description includes systems up to three variables and determinant use in its College Algebra description. The same page describes a separate Advanced Algebra course containing matrices/determinants, sequences, counting, the binomial theorem, induction, and partial fractions. [DLSU course descriptions](https://old.dlsu.edu.ph/colleges/gcoe/academic-departments/civil-engineering/course-description/)
- **Change:** The syllabus now discloses that CEStudy intentionally uses substitution and elimination for systems and excludes matrix/determinant methods and partial fractions to preserve the approved subject boundary.
- **Interpretation:** This is a CEStudy-specific learning sequence informed by published curricula, not an exact match to DLSU's course outline or a claim that every university assigns topics identically.

### A6. Readiness diagnostic must remain formative

- **Severity:** Medium, student progression
- **Evidence:** The approved design describes readiness as identifying gaps before the main course.
- **Change:** Retained explicit wording that the diagnostic recommends review and does not block progress.
- **Verification needed during implementation:** Feedback should show specific topics to review, not label learners as incapable or prevent access to the main material.

### A7. Assessment thresholds are not fixed yet

- **Severity:** Low at syllabus stage; required before assessment behavior is implemented
- **Evidence:** The syllabus is self-paced and intentionally has no university credit units, contact hours, grade percentages, or official pass mark.
- **Change:** No threshold was invented. The syllabus keeps diagnostic feedback, practice, unit checks, cumulative checks, and an integrated application.
- **Open decision:** When implementing assessment behavior, choose whether unit checks have a recommended mastery threshold and retries. This must be a CEStudy product decision, not presented as a CHED or university requirement.

## 4. Source checks

1. **De La Salle University, Civil Engineering Course Descriptions.** The public College Algebra description covers expressions, equations, inequalities, functions, polynomial/fractional/radical equations, exponential/logarithmic equations, and systems involving up to three variables. The same page lists Advanced Algebra separately. This supports the broad topic family while showing that exact topic boundaries differ by institution. [Source](https://old.dlsu.edu.ph/colleges/gcoe/academic-departments/civil-engineering/course-description/)
2. **CHED CMO No. 29, Series of 2007.** The historical BSCE curriculum table lists College Algebra and Advanced Algebra as separate courses, along with Trigonometry, Analytic Geometry, and Solid Mensuration. This is evidence of historical curriculum packaging, not a declaration that the CEStudy combination is an official syllabus. [Source PDF](https://legacy.ched.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/CMO-No.29-s2007.pdf)
3. **CHED CMO No. 92, Series of 2017.** Used as the later BSCE program-standards reference in the project source map. CEStudy's syllabus must not be described as CHED-approved or university-accredited.
